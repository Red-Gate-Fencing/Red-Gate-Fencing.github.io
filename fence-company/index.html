Searching for a fence company in Camp Hill or the Harrisburg area often starts with a material — wood, vinyl, aluminum — and ends with a more important question: who will plan the job, show up, and still answer the phone after the last post is set. A fence is a long-lived part of a property. The company you hire should be organized enough to handle the project as a relationship, not a one-day drop-off.

A fence company is more than a product list

Materials matter, but so do process and communication. A capable fence company should be able to walk a lot with you, explain why one style fits a backyard in Hampden Township and another fits a farm lane in New Bloomfield, and put the plan in writing. It should also be clear about who does the work. Red Gate Fencing, based at 118 Yellow Breeches Drive in Camp Hill, describes itself as a family-owned and operated business and says there is no rotating crew of subcontractors handling the work. That is a useful question to ask any company you consider: who will actually be on your property?

The company’s owners, Stephen and Erica Snyder, say they started Red Gate after years in construction and home improvement, having watched homeowners deal with poor communication, confusing estimates, and contractors who disappeared once a job was finished. They describe the standard they wanted for their own home: clear communication, straightforward pricing, quality installation, and a company that stands behind its work. When you evaluate a fence company, test those claims against how they answer the phone, how the estimate is written, and how they talk about callbacks.

How a local, family-owned company tends to run a job

Family ownership is not automatically a quality guarantee, but it does change how decisions get made. Red Gate’s about page says there is no corporate script and no pressure to upsell unnecessary add-ons, and that every project is supposed to get the same attention from the initial site visit through the final walkthrough. For a homeowner, the practical test is simple. Do they listen to what the fence needs to do? Do they explain options you did not ask for, or only the ones that solve the problem? Do they talk about maintenance honestly, including the upkeep a wood fence will need compared with vinyl or aluminum?

The company also says referrals and repeat customers have remained a major part of the business as it has grown. That is worth noticing in a local market like the West Shore and greater Harrisburg area, where neighbors compare notes. You can also read recent customer feedback on the Google Business Profile, which currently lists a 4.9-star rating from 54 reviews.

Materials and services a complete fence company should discuss

Red Gate Fencing’s service list covers the main residential, commercial, and agricultural options used in Central Pennsylvania:

  • Privacy fence installation in wood or vinyl, planned around the layout of the lot so a backyard can feel secluded from neighbors or passing traffic.
  • Wood fence installation, from classic picket lines that add curb appeal to taller privacy fences built with lumber chosen for local weather.
  • Vinyl fencing, often chosen when a homeowner wants a privacy or semi-privacy look with less long-term maintenance than wood.
  • Aluminum fence installation for a cleaner, lower-maintenance look along a front yard, pool, or decorative border.
  • Chain link fence installation with galvanized systems used for yards, play areas, pet containment, and commercial lots.
  • Farm fence installation planned around terrain, acreage, and livestock needs on rural properties.

A good company will not treat those as interchangeable. Vinyl and wood can both provide privacy, but they age and clean differently. Aluminum and chain link both leave more visibility. Farm fencing is a different design problem than a suburban backyard. Ask the company to recommend from your goals and your site, not from whatever is in stock that week.

Communication, estimates, and what happens after the fence is up
